AI & ML Research · Sep – Nov 2026 · final report 30 Nov

Card fraud below 1%

Tabular foundation models are said to match tuned tree models. Does that still hold when only 0.17% of transactions are fraud?

Reading the chart: as fraud gets rarer (left to right), every model loses ground. With a random context the foundation models end about 0.10 below XGBoost trained on all the data. Showing them balanced sets of frauds closes most of that gap (0.858 vs 0.865), so the rows you choose matter more than the model.

Case study: why do people share Spotify Wrapped?

Tap the right side for the next card. Hold to pause.

Wrapped comes out once a year and still gets one of the biggest spikes of use and free sharing in consumer tech. I wanted to know which design choices cause that, and where copying it would go wrong.

Five choices, and what each one causes

What I concluded

Engagement and virality are two different machines. Engagement happens inside the app and keeps people coming back. Virality happens outside it, runs on identity, and brings new people in.

Where it breaks

It needs months of personal data, some emotion, and sharing that feels safe. Most B2B tools, dull utilities, young products and anything about health or money don't have those.
